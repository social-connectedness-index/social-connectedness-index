Social Connectedness Index

The Social Connectedness Index (SCI) is a globally comparable measure of the relative probability that Facebook users in two locations are friends.

What the number means

The SCI answers a simple question: if you pick one person in one place and another person somewhere else, how likely are they to be friends?

The SCI is relative.

If one SCI value is twice another, the friendship likelihood is twice as high. The exact units matter less than ratios and patterns.

The SCI is symmetric.

The connection from A to B is the same as the connection from B to A, because Facebook friendships are mutual links.

The SCI is privacy protected.

The public data are aggregated and differentially private. The tools never expose person-level friendship records.

Why it matters

By leveraging social network data, the SCI overcomes longstanding measurement challenges and enables new lines of research on the role of social networks in determining economic, social, and political outcomes.

Markets & trade

Social connectedness is an important determinant of international and subnational trade flows.

Health & mobility

The SCI has shown that the geographic spread of COVID-19 correlates with social network structure.

Migration flows

Friendship networks can reveal where people may have family, friends, or support systems when they move or are displaced.

Social clusters

Administrative borders are not always social borders. SCI clusters show which regions behave like connected communities.
